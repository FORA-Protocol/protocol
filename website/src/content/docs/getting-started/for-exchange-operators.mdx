An Exchange holds publishers' catalogs and terms, signs their offers, records each sale and takes usage reports. Anyone can run one. FORA Server, which is open source, includes the Exchange, and every FORA agent can buy from any FORA Exchange, because every Exchange speaks the same protocol. You set your own rules and your own fee.

Settlement stays outside the protocol: you bill the agents' account holders and pay your publishers through your own billing. FORA defines no payment rail.

## Who runs an Exchange

The same messages carry any catalog. What changes is the unit an offer is metered in and the domain profile its metadata follows:

| Exchange over | What it holds, and from whom | Who might run it |
|---|---|---|
| News | Articles, podcasts and video, from publishers | A news publisher group, a press agency |
| Scholarly content | Journal articles and preprints, from publishers and repositories | A university press, a society publisher |
| Legal records | Court filings, legislation and patents, from courts and patent offices | A legal-data company |
| Financial data | Credit reports and market data, from data providers | A data vendor |
| Medical data | Clinical data and imaging, from hospitals and research institutions | A research consortium |
| Public records | Property records and business filings, from agencies | A public-records company |
| Music | Recordings and the works they record, from labels, publishers and collectives | A licensing collective |

An existing marketplace can run a FORA Exchange over its own catalog, beside the channels it already has. See [For Content Marketplaces](/getting-started/existing-marketplace/).

## What you deploy

FORA Server's Exchange is one Go binary in a container, `ghcr.io/fora-protocol/exchange`. It listens on two ports: the public Exchange listener, `8081` by default, and an internal admin listener, `8082`. To run it you provide:

- **Two PostgreSQL databases**: one for the catalog, the transaction log and the audit log (`EXCHANGE_DSN`), and one for the account registry (`EXCHANGE_SOR_DSN`).
- **Redis** (`REDIS_URL`), which stops a signed request from being replayed. A single instance can run without it and then keeps this record in its own memory. Every instance of a scaled-out Exchange must share one Redis.
- **An Ed25519 private key**, which signs offers and delivery URLs. An RSA key is needed only for a publisher whose CDN is AWS CloudFront.
- **A ledger.** `FORA_BILLING_ADAPTER` chooses it: `tigerbeetle` keeps a prepaid balance per agent account in TigerBeetle, in the currencies `EXCHANGE_BILLING_LEDGER` lists. The default, `free`, approves every charge and records none.
- **The Broker's address** (`EXCHANGE_BROKER_WELLKNOWN_URL`). The Exchange reads the Broker's withdrawn-key list from there and refuses to start without the address. See [Broker](/components/broker/overview/#who-runs-a-broker).
- **Your hostname** (`EXCHANGE_DOMAIN`), and a seat for that hostname. A seat holds your fee and your agent policy.

The catalog importer, `fora-import`, ships in the same image and runs as a job. The publisher console is a separate, optional service. You can deploy each component by hand, or with the Terraform modules in the FORA Server repository, which render a Docker Compose stack. The [Exchange deployment guide](https://github.com/FORA-Protocol/reference-implementation/blob/main/src/exchange/DEPLOYMENT.md) and the [runbook](https://github.com/FORA-Protocol/reference-implementation/blob/main/src/exchange/RUNBOOK.md) are in the FORA Server repository.

The Exchange answers these calls:

| Call | What it does |
|---|---|
| `ExchangeService.Register` | Opens an agent's account and returns its `billing_ref` |
| `ExchangeService.GetAccountStatus` | Returns the state of the agent's account |
| `ExchangeService.DiscoverResources` | Returns signed offers for the URLs in a `ResourceQuery` |
| `ExchangeService.ExecuteTransaction` | Buys offers and returns a signed delivery URL for each item it grants |
| `ExchangeService.ReportUsage` | Takes the agent's usage report and returns its `report_id` |
| `CatalogService.PushResources` | Takes catalog entries from a contributor the site's `fora.json` names |
| `AdminService.SetTenantFeeRate` | Sets your fee, on the admin listener only |
| `AdminService.SetReportingPolicy` | Sets your reporting policy, on the admin listener only |

It also serves `/.well-known/fora.json`, your key directory at `/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory`, `/healthz` and `/readyz`.

## Your rules and your fee

- **Your fee.** Your commission is `fee_rate_bps` on a seat, in basis points, set with `AdminService.SetTenantFeeRate` without a restart. 1500 is 15%. With the `tigerbeetle` ledger, each sale posts the gross, your fee and the publisher's net. The `free` ledger records none of them.
- **Which agents can buy.** `activate_new_agents_by_default` decides whether a newly registered agent can buy at once, or waits until you activate its account. A deactivated account is refused paid purchases with `DENIAL_REASON_ACCOUNT_INACTIVE`.
- **A welcome credit.** A new account can start with a balance. `EXCHANGE_DEFAULT_AGENT_CREDIT` sets the default amounts, for example `EUR=100`. Each currency must be one that your ledger bills in.
- **Purchases through a Broker.** `allow_broker_relay` on a seat decides whether you accept purchases the Broker relays. The protocol denies each refused item in the body with `DENIAL_REASON_RELAY_NOT_ACCEPTED`. FORA Server's Exchange does not do this: it refuses the whole relayed purchase, with that reason in `ErrorDetail.transaction_denial`. Either way, the agent can buy the offers directly.
- **How many signatures a request may carry.** `EXCHANGE_MAX_INTERMEDIARY_HOPS`, published as `max_intermediary_hops`, 4 by default.
- **Reporting.** `AdminService.SetReportingPolicy` sets which fields a usage report must carry and how long the agent has to send it. Its deprecated `quantity_tolerance` is accepted and ignored: the protocol never refuses a report for the quantity it states, and neither does FORA Server's Exchange. See [After the purchase](/components/exchange/overview/#after-the-purchase).

The publishers set the terms and prices of their own content. Your Exchange signs the offers it builds from them.

## Publishers

A publisher joins your Exchange through a publisher console:

1. It signs in and registers its business and its site.
2. It sets its terms. The console fills them in from the site's RSL license when the site publishes one, and the publisher sets the price.
3. The console generates the site's `fora.json`, which names your Exchange and lists your catalog contributor (`EXCHANGE_CATALOG_CONTRIBUTOR_HOST`) in `catalog_contributors`, and the edge function package for the site's CDN. The publisher serves the file and installs the package, usually in its own CDN account.
4. The console checks both, and the listing goes live once the site proves it authorized your Exchange. `fora-import` then imports the pages of the site's sitemap into your catalog, with the publisher's terms, through `CatalogService.PushResources`.

From then on, the publisher sees its sales in the console. See [Publisher Onboarding](/getting-started/publisher-onboarding/) for the publisher's side.

## Agents

An agent opens an account at your Exchange with `ExchangeService.Register`. The Exchange identifies the agent by the key directory its request signature names, never by the request body, and returns a `billing_ref`, the opaque, long-lived handle of the agent's account at your Exchange. Registering again returns the same handle. A paid purchase without an account is denied with `DENIAL_REASON_ACCOUNT_NOT_REGISTERED`; free content needs none.

You don't need a separate deal with each AI company. Any agent that speaks FORA can open an account and buy, under your agent policy. An agent on MCP registers through the [Identity Service](/components/identity/overview/).

## Requester verification

The `Requester` message carries no signature: it states who is asking, and the RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signature on the request proves who sent it. An Exchange MUST verify every request before acting on it, and FORA Server's Exchange does. The rules are on the [Authentication](/protocol/authentication/) page. An Exchange applies them in this order, and rejects a request that fails any of them, with no fallback and no degraded mode:

1. **Count the signatures first.** Before you verify any, refuse a request carrying more signatures than your `WellKnownManifest.max_intermediary_hops` with `resource_exhausted` (HTTP 429) and no typed reason. Every signature counts, whether or not it covers another. See [Verification at the Exchange](/protocol/authentication/#verification-at-the-exchange).
2. **Verify each signature under the Web Bot Auth profile.** Resolve its `keyid`, the key's RFC 7638 thumbprint, in the key directory its own covered `Signature-Agent` member names (`{origin}/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory`), never from `Requester.domain`. Check the profile parameters, the four components a FORA RPC covers (`@method`, `@target-uri`, `content-digest`, `authorization`) and the `Content-Digest` of the body. Refuse a failure as `unauthenticated` with `request_auth_failure`: `SIGNATURE_MISSING` when no signature parses, `SIGNATURE_INVALID` when one does not verify, `SIGNATURE_STALE` outside its `created`/`expires` window. When a required component is missing, also answer with an `Accept-Signature` field listing the components you require, so the client can fix its request. See [Signature Verification](/protocol/authentication/#signature-verification) and [The Web Bot Auth Profile](/protocol/authentication/#the-web-bot-auth-profile).
3. **Refuse a replay.** A signature you have already accepted is refused as `SIGNATURE_STALE`. See [Replay protection](/protocol/authentication/#replay-protection).
4. **Check revocation against the signer's own directory only.** A key is revoked when the revocation list its own directory names lists it. No other party's list can revoke it, even one that names its thumbprint. See [Whose list revokes whose key](/protocol/authentication/#whose-list-revokes-whose-key). Poll that list only when the directory's `revocation_url` sits on the directory's own host and port, or a subdomain of it; see [Emergency Revocation](/protocol/authentication/#emergency-revocation).
5. **Tell a direct request from a relayed one.** On `ResourceQuery` and `TransactionRequest`, compare `Requester.domain` with the host of the directory the covered `Signature-Agent` member names, by the request-recipient identity rule. Equal is a direct request from the requester itself. Different is a request a Broker authored for that requester, and you act on it only when you admit the signer as a Broker, by your own policy (for example, the Brokers registered with you); otherwise refuse it as `unauthenticated` with `request_auth_failure` `SIGNATURE_INVALID`. A Broker never forwards an agent's request unchanged: it signs the requests it authors alone. See [Direct and relayed requests](/protocol/authentication/#direct-and-relayed-requests).
6. **On a purchase, verify the agent's acceptances.** Verify each item's `AgentAcceptance`, and the request's `AgentRequestAcceptance`, against the Ed25519 keys currently valid in the directory `Requester.domain` names, never against a Broker's key. On a relayed purchase these are the agent's identity and consent, the acceptance key is what the delivery URL is bound to, and a delegation's `cnf.jkt` is checked against it. A sub-request that is not the exact in-order projection of the set the agent signed is denied in the body, every item with `DENIAL_REASON_SIGNATURE_INVALID`. See [Projected execute requests](/protocol/authentication/#projected-execute-requests).
7. **On Broker-led discovery, `Requester.domain` is the Broker's statement.** A `ResourceQuery` a Broker originated carries no agent signature, so nothing authenticates the field there: it says whom the Broker queries for, under the Broker's own signature.

Cache directories and keys with a TTL and re-fetch them to pick up rotation, without a fetch per request (see [Caching Contract](/protocol/authentication/#caching-contract)).

### Delegation verification

When a request includes a `Delegation` message (nested inside `Requester`), the Exchange runs the chain through the profile's verifier (`helpers.VerifyDelegation` in the Go SDK). The procedure, the limits and the checks are stated once, in [Verification](/protocol/authentication/#verification). This page adds only what the Exchange supplies to the verifier and what it does with the result:

1. **Trust comes from the subscription record.** Give the verifier the trusted root keys of the subscription record that owns the gated term, each with the issuer identity it belongs to. Trust is configured on that record, never fetched: not from `principal_domain`, not from `issuer`, not from a WBA directory, OIDC or JWKS. A key trusted on one record grants nothing on another. Withdraw trust by removing a key from the record's list; `revocation_uri` is not part of the profile.
2. **The holder is the key the request proved.** Give the verifier the thumbprint of that key: on a direct request the key that signed it (RFC 9421), on a purchase a Broker relays the key that signed each item's `AgentAcceptance`. The verifier refuses a mismatch. This is mandatory: it is what makes a stolen token unusable, so a token possessed without its signing key grants nothing. On a query a Broker originated, which proves possession of no agent key, do not verify or honor the delegation: answer as if none were present.
3. **One outcome per failure.** A `token` over 32768 bytes fails wire validation: the whole request is refused as invalid, before any item is read. At discovery, a chain the verifier refuses is treated as if no delegation were present: the gated terms stay hidden and no denial reason reports why; you MAY journal the refusal. At purchase, a chain the verifier refuses denies that item with `DENIAL_REASON_DELEGATION_INVALID`.
4. **Use what the verifier returns:** the root issuer, the holder thumbprint, the effective scopes and the effective expiry. A gated offer you issue MUST NOT expire later than that effective expiry. A chain that carries a cap claim (`fora_max_spend_cents`, `fora_max_accesses`, `fora_quota_period`) is refused by this release's verifier; meter subscriptions on the record, as [Subscription Quota Signaling](#subscription-quota-signaling) describes.

This verification is entirely offline — no callback to the principal is required.

### Scope-based catalog filtering

After verifying identity (and any delegation, as above), present the non-gated terms the requester's stated scopes cover; only those reach the `DiscoverResources` response. The scopes are the requester's own statement and you do not verify them. A gated term, a subscription term in the usual case, is different: present it only when the verified delegation's effective scopes cover it, ignore `requester.scopes` for that term, and answer a requester without a verified delegation as if the term did not exist. The matching rule is stated once, in [Scope Matching](/protocol/authentication/#scope-matching).

For a resource that exists but falls outside the requester's scopes, answer exactly as for a resource you have nothing to offer for: an `OfferGroup` with no offers and `absence_reason` unset. Do not reveal that the resource exists, and do not send a reason that tells the two cases apart. This applies to enterprise RBAC and public marketplace subscriptions alike (see [Existence hiding](/protocol/authentication/#existence-hiding)).

An offer you presented stays purchasable until it expires, so a purchase is never refused because the requester's scopes do not cover the resource.

### Subscription quota signaling

When the requester holds a metered subscription, an Exchange can report the allowance left in `SubscriptionQuotaInfo` entries on offers and transaction responses, so the agent does not commit to a purchase it cannot complete. An Exchange meters a subscription on its subscription record, never per delegation token, and names the record in `SubscriptionQuotaInfo.subscription_id`.

### Denial reasons

FORA defines identity- and entitlement-related denial reasons an Exchange MUST return when they apply. The delegation reasons:

| Denial Reason | When to Return |
|---|---|
| `DENIAL_REASON_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` | Subscription access count exhausted for the current `quota_period` |
| `DENIAL_REASON_DELEGATION_INVALID` | On a purchase item only: the delegation is missing, or the verifier refuses the chain (malformed, chain linkage, issuer or signature, expired or not yet valid by the signed chain, another recipient, holder binding, a child that widens its parent, or scopes or caps that do not cover the request). Never at discovery, where the gated terms are hidden instead, and never for an oversized token, which wire validation refuses. See [Verification](/protocol/authentication/#verification) |

And the entitlement/subscription family, for subscription-gated offers:

| Denial Reason | When to Return |
|---|---|
| `DENIAL_REASON_ENTITLEMENT_MISSING` | No entitlement token presented for a subscription-only offer |
| `DENIAL_REASON_ENTITLEMENT_MALFORMED` | Entitlement token failed to decode |
| `DENIAL_REASON_ENTITLEMENT_EXPIRED` | Entitlement token's validity window has passed |
| `DENIAL_REASON_ENTITLEMENT_WRONG_BUYER` | Token's `subscriber_org` does not match the asserted requester |
| `DENIAL_REASON_SUBSCRIPTION_LAPSED` | The covering subscription contract has lapsed |
| `DENIAL_REASON_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` | Subscription exists but no buyer-side grant ties this caller to it |

And the relay reason, for a provider that does not accept purchases a Broker relays:

| Denial Reason | When to Return |
|---|---|
| `DENIAL_REASON_RELAY_NOT_ACCEPTED` | The provider that sells this item's resource does not accept purchases relayed by a Broker. Decided per item by each offer's provider and answered in the body, never as a refusal of the whole sub-request; the agent can buy the offer directly |

FORA Server's Exchange does not follow this rule: it refuses the whole relayed request, as [Your rules and your fee](#your-rules-and-your-fee) describes.

This is the identity/entitlement subset with operator guidance; the complete, authoritative `DenialReason` enum is rendered from the proto on the [proto reference](/reference/proto-fora/). For the full identity model specification — Requester fields, the JWT chain, scope format, and key management — see [Authentication](/protocol/authentication/).

## Units and profiles

An offer names what it is metered in, and a metered purchase charges the publisher's estimate times the rate:

- `unit`: what is metered.
- `rate`: the price of one `unit`.
- `estimated_quantity`: optional, the publisher's own estimate, in `unit`. A metered purchase charges the estimate × `rate`, or one unit's `rate` when the offer states no estimate, and that charge is final (see [Charging a metered purchase](/protocol/transaction-flow/#charging-a-metered-purchase)).
- `unit_cost`: optional, the Exchange's normalized cost per unit, in its `base_currency`, for comparing offers across Exchanges (for text, the cost per token). It never enters the charge.

FORA Server's Exchange does not follow the charge rule: it charges the offer's `unit_cost`, or its `rate` when `unit_cost` is not set, times the estimate. The two agree when an offer has no `unit_cost` or its `unit_cost` equals its `rate`. See [Billing and Ledgers](/components/exchange/billing-adapter/#charging-flow).

```
Exchange over       Pricing.unit                Example rate
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
News                tokens                      $0.00003/token
Legal records       pages                       $0.50/page
Scholarly content   accesses                    $0.05/access
Medical imaging     images                      $2.00/image
Financial data      records                     $5.00/record
Geospatial          sq-km                       $0.10/sq-km
Audio streams       minutes                     $0.02/minute
Drug interactions   pharma:interaction_pairs    $0.001/pair
```

A usage report states `consumed_quantity` and `consumed_unit`. `consumed_unit` must match the offer's `unit`, and a report that omits it is read as tokens. Neither the protocol nor FORA Server's Exchange refuses a report for the quantity it reports.

Your manifest's `supported_profiles` tells Brokers and agents which domain profiles your offers follow. FORA Server's Exchange declares `fora-news-v1`, `fora-comp-v1` and `fora-music-v1`. An agent declares the profiles it understands, and a Broker forwards that list to your Exchange unchanged. See [Extension Profiles](/protocol/extension-profiles/).

## Exchange manifest and health

Your Exchange serves its manifest at `/.well-known/fora.json`, with `role` set to `ROLE_EXCHANGE`. It tells Brokers and agents what your Exchange offers: its endpoint, `base_currency`, `supported_profiles` and `max_intermediary_hops`. In FORA Server, with the `tigerbeetle` ledger, `base_currency` is the first currency in `EXCHANGE_BILLING_LEDGER`; with the `free` or `inmemory` ledger, it is `USD`. The Exchange rebuilds the manifest from its configuration. Your offer-signing keys are in your key directory, not in `fora.json`. See [Exchange Manifest](/protocol/exchange-manifest/).

`/healthz` checks the catalog database, and `/readyz` also checks the ledger. FORA Server's Broker reads the health endpoint of each Exchange in its registry and routes only to an Exchange that answers `200`.

## Attestations

An attestation is a signed claim about a resource, by its publisher or by a verification vendor. When attestations arrive with `CatalogService.PushResources`, the protocol has the Exchange check that the signer is the provider itself or a contributor the provider's `catalog_contributors` lists, and verify the attestation's Ed25519 signature against the key in the signer's key directory. See [Resource Attestation](/protocol/content-attestation/).

FORA Server's Exchange does not check attestations: it stores the attestations a push carries and returns them on offers without verifying their signatures.

## Usage reports and disputes

`ExchangeService.ReportUsage` takes a report on a transaction your Exchange recorded and returns a `report_id`. An agent files a dispute with `ExchangeService.DisputeTransaction` at the Exchange that sold the item, and each `DisputeRequest` names the `report_id` of its usage report. The protocol standardizes the dispute's states and outcomes; how an Exchange decides a dispute is up to the Exchange. See [Dispute Resolution](/protocol/dispute-resolution/).

FORA Server's Exchange does not implement `DisputeTransaction`: the call returns `unimplemented`. It stores the evidence of each sale, and disputes are handled outside the platform.

## Brokers

An agent can buy from your Exchange directly or through a [Broker](/components/broker/overview/). A Broker sends your Exchange only the URLs it routes to you, never the agent's words, and returns your signed offers unchanged, each as a full package, in its own order of relevance. The agent decides which to buy. Your account holder is always the agent: a purchase through a Broker charges the agent's account at your Exchange, never the Broker's.
