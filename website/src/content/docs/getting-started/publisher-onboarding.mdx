The publisher console takes your site from registration to its first sale. You tell it your domain and your terms, and it generates the two things that run on your side: a discovery file and an edge package for your CDN. You place one file and one function. From then on, AI agents pay for your pages and your readers are unaffected.

You generate no keys and you never push a catalog. The operator, the company that runs the console and the Exchange, imports your pages from your sitemap. Registering costs nothing. Start at the <a href={FORA_PUBLISHER_CONSOLE_URL}>publisher console</a>.

## Before you start

- Your domain is served by Cloudflare or AWS CloudFront, the two networks the console sets up. For any other network, see [If your network is not supported](#if-your-network-is-not-supported).
- You can publish a file at `/.well-known/fora.json` on that domain.
- You can deploy to your CDN account: a Worker on Cloudflare, or a Lambda@Edge function on CloudFront.
- Your site has a sitemap, at `/sitemap.xml` or named on a `Sitemap:` line in `robots.txt`. The console finds it by itself. Without one, your pages are not imported automatically.

## Create your account

<LinkButton href={FORA_PUBLISHER_CONSOLE_URL}>Register on the publisher console</LinkButton>

The console sends you to its sign-in page first, and that page also lets you create an account. Register with an email address and a password, or with a Google account.

An account made with an email address needs confirming. Open the message you receive, follow the link, then sign in again. Until you do, the console shows only "Please confirm your email address". A Google account arrives already confirmed.

## Add your domain

Once you are signed in, the console offers "Add your first domain". Press "Add domain" to start. The setup has five steps: "Your business", "Your domain", "Publish a file", "Install the package" and "Set your terms".

<Aside type="note">
Nothing about your domain is saved until you press "Finish setup" in the last step. Only your business name is kept along the way.
</Aside>

### Your business

The console asks for your business name once, before your first domain. It is the name your payments are made out to, and you cannot change it in the console later.

### Your domain

Enter the hostname only, such as `www.example.com`, with no `https://` in front and no path after it, then press "Check". The console works out which CDN serves the domain, because that decides how the rest of the setup goes.

Under "Licensed paths", list the parts of the domain you license, separated by commas, like `/articles/, /archive/`. Leave the field empty to license the whole domain.

If the domain is already registered to another publisher, the console says so. If it is yours, ask the operator to transfer it.

### Publish a file

The console generates your `fora.json`. Download it and serve it, unchanged, at `https://<your-domain>/.well-known/fora.json`, then press "Check".

The file proves that the domain is yours. It also names the Exchange that sells access to your pages, the account that gets paid, and the operator's catalog contributor, which imports your pages (see [Keys and your catalog](#keys-and-your-catalog)). The [fora.json example](/reference/fora-json-example/) explains each field.

The check reads the file from your domain. It does not test delivery through your CDN; the next step checks part of that. If the file does not match yet, the console says why. The fix is always the same: serve the downloaded file exactly as it is, then press "Check again".

### Install the package

The package is the one function you place. It runs in front of your licensed paths: it lets people and search crawlers through, refuses AI agents that have no license, and accepts an agent that comes back with the signed address the Exchange gave it. The console builds it for your domain, with your settings already in it. It holds no secret; it fetches the Exchange's public keys.

The console shows the commands for your network.

#### Cloudflare

Download `fora-cloudflare.zip`. It holds the Worker and its `wrangler.toml`, with your settings and your route already filled in. Unpack it and deploy it with Wrangler.

<Aside type="caution">
Keep your DNS records proxied through Cloudflare, or requests never reach the Worker.
</Aside>

#### AWS CloudFront

Download `fora-lambda-edge.zip`. It holds the function, with your settings beside it, because Lambda@Edge takes no environment variables. Then:

1. Create the function from the package in the `us-east-1` region, with a role that Lambda@Edge can assume.
2. Publish a version of it. CloudFront attaches a published version, never `$LATEST`.
3. In your distribution, edit the cache behavior for your licensed paths and add that version as a viewer-request association.

<Aside type="caution">
Leave the distribution's own signed-URL check off. The function does the checking.
</Aside>

#### Check the package

Once the package is deployed, the console checks your live site, asking both as an AI agent and as an ordinary browser. It confirms three things: an agent without a license is refused, people and search crawlers are let through, and your discovery document is still served. You can go on once the package is confirmed and no check has failed. These checks do not complete a paid delivery.

If your discovery document stops answering after you deploy, the package's routes have to cover the well-known paths as well.

### Set your terms

Choose what an AI agent may do with your pages and what it pays. This is what an agent is offered when it asks for one of them.

- Price: "Free" or "Charge". Charge fills in a default price per unit, in the Exchange's currency, and you can change it.
- Uses: "Search" (listing and linking your pages), "AI input" (answers and retrieval, quoting your pages) and "AI training" (training a model on your pages).
- Attribution: "Required" means every use must credit you.

If your site publishes an RSL licensing file, the form opens on the terms it states. Otherwise it opens on free, with search and AI input allowed and AI training not.

Pages of your domain that were in the catalog before automatic import, for example from a feed, keep their own terms unless you tick "Replace the terms of pages that are already in the catalog". Their titles and descriptions are kept either way.

Press "Finish setup". The console reads your file once more and saves the domain. The domain's page then shows your terms and "Your setup files", which you can download again. The console does not let you change the terms yet.

## After setup

The operator imports your pages on a schedule. Each run reads your sitemap and lists the pages on your domain that fall within your licensed paths, with the terms you set. A page whose address carries a query string is left out. Imported pages appear under "Catalog" in the console; until the first import has run, it says "Nothing is listed yet."

The rest of the console shows what your pages earn:

- "Overview" totals your sales for a period: transactions, gross, the exchange fee and net.
- "Transactions" lists each sale, filterable by domain and by usage-report status. On a sale's page, "Verify" checks both Ed25519 signatures in your browser: the Exchange's on the offer and the agent's on its acceptance.
- "Income" shows daily totals, with "Download CSV".
- "My domains" lists your domains and your terms with the Exchange, such as its fee. The operator sets those.

## Keys and your catalog

You generate no keys and upload none, and you never push your catalog yourself. The operator holds the catalog contributor key that imports your pages, and your `fora.json` authorizes the operator's contributor to do that for your domain. Keep the file as the console generated it: removing that authorization stops the import, and nothing on the Exchange side can restore it. The edge package holds no secret either; it fetches the Exchange's public keys.

## If your network is not supported

The console sets up sites served by Cloudflare or AWS CloudFront. For any other network, or when it cannot tell which network serves your domain, it stops at the domain step and shows an address to write to. A site behind a service that hides its origin can end up here too, so write even if you do use one of the two.

## Without a publisher console

A publisher console is the operator's tool, not part of the protocol. Where an operator runs FORA without one, a publisher sets up the same pieces directly:

- The discovery file: the [fora.json example](/reference/fora-json-example/) and the field reference in [Exchange Manifest](/protocol/exchange-manifest/).
- The edge function: [Edge function deployment](/components/edge-function/deployment/).
- The catalog, pushed with a contributor key that the publisher's `fora.json` authorizes: [Pushing with the Publisher SDK](/components/content-ingestion/sources/#pushing-with-the-publisher-sdk) and the [JSONL Ingestion Feed](/protocol/jsonl-ingestion/).
- Self-attestation with the publisher's own signing key: [Content Attestation](/protocol/content-attestation/).
