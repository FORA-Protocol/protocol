## Overview

The **Universal Licensing Core** is FORA's model for the commercial licensing terms of any digital resource. A resource carries zero or more `LicenseTerm` entries, at most 32. Each term is a complete, self-contained access arrangement, and a resource with several terms is the normal case.

The same `LicenseTerm` shape appears at both ends of the pipeline:

- **Ingestion** (`ResourceEntry.terms`). A publisher declares terms when it pushes resources into an Exchange catalog with `CatalogService.PushResources`. A term in `ResourceEntry.terms` is a *catalog term*.
- **Emission** (`Offer.terms`). An *offer* is a signed `Offer` that the Exchange presents for exactly one catalog term. The Exchange moves the term's price into `Offer.pricing` and carries the rest of the term on the offer, with each restriction token in its canonical form (see [Restrictions](#restrictions)) and no other translation or reformulation.

The Exchange presents a term when the scopes the requester states cover the term's `LicenseTerm.scopes`. For a term the Exchange gates behind a delegation, it presents the term only when a verified delegation grants those scopes (see [Scope-Based Access](/protocol/authentication/#scope-based-access)). A term with no `scopes` is public. Unless the Exchange pre-filters (see [Restrictions](#restrictions)), a resource with three presented terms yields three offers, one per term.

An offer states its price **once**, in `Offer.pricing`. The term inside the offer carries no `pricing` (rule `offer.terms.pricing_unset`). Each offer derives from exactly one catalog term, so the offer's price and the term's price cannot disagree, and `ExecuteTransaction` charges `Offer.pricing`.

## Why multiple terms per resource?

A single resource often has different access arrangements for different uses. For example:

- A news article is free for academic RAG but costs $0.05 per access for commercial entities.
- A stock photo is licensed for commercial display (paid, with a cap of 500,000 impressions).
- A patent design file is licensed for viewing ($0) or for manufacturing (a flat fee for up to 1,000 units, with self-reported production).

One resource entry, several terms, each covering a complete and distinct access arrangement.

## Term semantics

Every `LicenseTerm` has a `TermSemantics` value.

`Pricing` is required on **every catalog term**, whatever its semantics: a catalog term without `Pricing` is refused (rule `resource_entry.terms.pricing_required`), and a free term states `model: FREE` explicitly, because absent pricing does not mean free. An agent cannot act on a term without a price, so the machine-readable price is always present, even when the rest of the term is in a document. On an offer, that price is `Offer.pricing`, and the term the offer carries has none.

**`ENUMERATED`**: the machine fields (`restrictions`, `quotas`, `obligations`) are the complete, authoritative expression of the term. They are checked at ingest: no token may be both permitted and prohibited on one axis, a term carries at most one restriction per kind, and the cross-field rules on `Pricing`, `License` and `Obligation` apply. The same checks ship in the SDKs as a publisher pre-check (`ValidateResourceEntry` and `NormalizeLicenseTerm` in Go, and their TypeScript and Python equivalents).

Restrictions ride on every offer. They are not an Exchange-side gate that a requester must pass to see or buy a term: they are declared terms the parties agree to when the agent accepts the offer, and the Exchange never enforces them. The agent chooses the offer whose restrictions it can honor and is responsible for complying with them. So if a term prohibits `ai-train`, that prohibition rides on the offer and binds the transaction the agent accepts. An Exchange or Broker MAY, as a convenience, drop offers whose restrictions fall outside the limits the query states in `ResourceQuery.acceptable_restrictions`. It MAY report this with `OFFER_ABSENCE_REASON_RESTRICTION_FILTERED`, with the filtered axes in `OfferGroup.restriction_filters`. That filter is advisory, not enforcement.

**`REFERENCE_ONLY`**: the document at `License.uri` is the authoritative, complete source for `restrictions`, `quotas` and `obligations`, and the agent must read it before it uses the resource. A `REFERENCE_ONLY` term without a `License.uri` is refused. The machine fields are **optional** in this mode, and the publisher may send `Pricing` alone. Any `restrictions`, `quotas` or `obligations` the publisher does include must not contradict the referenced document, and they bind the parties exactly as under `ENUMERATED`. `Pricing` is still stated machine-readably. This mode is for license arrangements whose terms cannot be fully enumerated in machine-readable form, such as music rights, patent portfolios and collective management agreements.

**`License.uri_digest`**: every `License` that sets `uri` must also set `uri_digest`, whatever the term's semantics (rule `license.digest_required_with_uri`). The party that sets `uri` supplies the digest: the publisher, on a term it pushes. The digest has the form `method:hexdigest`, where the method is `sha256`, `sha384` or `sha512` and the hex digest has the method's full length. `md5` and `sha1` are refused on the wire. The digest pins the document, so an agent can check that the bytes it fetched are the ones the offer was signed with.

:::caution[Fetching `License.uri` safely]
`License.uri` MUST NOT be URL-validated, because TDL identifiers use non-URL schemes. That means *do not reject* a non-URL scheme, **not** *fetch blindly*. A consumer that dereferences the URI MUST apply the SSRF countermeasures in the [threat model (T-LIC-1)](/security/threat-model/#t-lic-1-ssrf--phishing-via-licenseuri): a scheme allowlist; blocking loopback, link-local, private and cloud-metadata addresses, resolving before checking; fetching client-side through an egress proxy; and treating the response as untrusted. It verifies the fetched bytes against `License.uri_digest` and refuses the document on a mismatch.
:::

## Restrictions

A `Restriction` constrains one licensing dimension:

| Kind | What it constrains | Vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| `FUNCTION` | What the agent may do with the resource | `(fora.v1.vocab_enum)` on `RESTRICTION_KIND_FUNCTION` |
| `GEOGRAPHY` | Where the agent is operating | `(fora.v1.vocab_enum)` on `RESTRICTION_KIND_GEOGRAPHY` |
| `USER_TYPE` | What kind of entity the agent represents | `(fora.v1.vocab_enum)` on `RESTRICTION_KIND_USER_TYPE` |
| `OTHER` | A custom axis, whose meaning is carried by its tokens | None: every bare token produces a warning, so use namespaced tokens |

The token lists are authored as proto-native buf options on the `RestrictionKind` enum values, with no side-car JSON registry. The `functiontokens`, `geographytokens` and `usertypes` constant packages and their `IsRegistered` checks are generated from them.

Each restriction carries:

- `permitted[]`: tokens allowed on this axis. An empty list permits any value.
- `prohibited[]`: tokens blocked on this axis. It takes precedence over `permitted[]`.
- `advisory`: when `false` (the **default**), the restriction is **binding**: an agent that cannot evaluate every token in it, including an unknown namespaced token, MUST decline the term. Set `advisory: true` to make a restriction the agent cannot evaluate non-blocking.

**A token cannot be both permitted and prohibited** on one axis, and that is checked twice, over two readings of the same lists. At the RPC boundary, `restriction.permitted_prohibited_disjoint` compares the tokens **as written**, because it runs over the request exactly as received. At ingest, `restriction.canonical_disjoint` compares the tokens after canonicalization. The second check is not a repetition: several tokens have more than one accepted spelling, so `permitted: ["scrape"]` with `prohibited: ["crawl"]`, or `["us"]` against `["US"]`, names one token on both sides while passing the first check. Either failure refuses the whole `PushResources` submission, and the Exchange stores no entry from it.

**Reading a restriction**: a value is in scope when `permitted[]` is empty or the value matches a token in it, and the value matches no token in `prohibited[]`. A term carries at most one restriction per kind (rule `license_term.one_restriction_per_kind`) and at most 8 restrictions. Its restrictions are AND-combined: the agent must satisfy all of them. The agent applies this rule itself when it chooses between the offers a resource yields.

On the wire, each token in `permitted[]` or `prohibited[]` is 1 to 64 characters from `A–Z`, `a–z`, `0–9`, `.`, `_`, `:`, `*` and `-`, and each list holds at most 64 tokens, so whitespace and non-ASCII characters are refused before ingest.

At ingest, the Exchange canonicalizes each token before it checks registry membership: it trims RFC 8259 whitespace, folds ASCII case (to lowercase for `FUNCTION` and `USER_TYPE`, to uppercase for `GEOGRAPHY`), and resolves the accepted aliases listed above to their registered token. It never folds a non-ASCII byte, because a Unicode fold would turn a homograph into a registered token. So a case variant or a listed alias never counts as an unknown token. Trimming and the non-ASCII rule matter only to a deployment that does not apply the wire rules.

An unregistered bare token produces a `PushResourcesResponse.warnings[]` entry (rule `restriction.token.registered`), and the term is accepted. A namespaced token, one that contains `:`, is never checked against the registry. This lets publishers use new vocabulary before the registry is updated. Because a restriction is binding unless it sets `advisory: true`, an agent that cannot evaluate such a token must decline the term.

## Quotas

A `Quota` is a usage cap the term declares: how much the agent may consume under the term before the term expires or must be renegotiated. Like a restriction, it is a declared term the parties agree to, and the Exchange does not enforce it. Quotas are not billing quantities; `Pricing` states what the agent pays.

Each quota has:
- `metric`: the unit being capped, either a registered token from the `(fora.v1.vocab)` option on `Quota.metric` or a namespaced custom token such as `vendor:productions`.
- `limit`: the maximum value in the given window, at least 1.
- `window`: `HOURLY`, `DAILY`, `MONTHLY` or `TOTAL` (a lifetime cap that never resets). A window runs from the time of the transaction, or, for a subscription term, from the start of the subscription.

**Standard metric tokens** (rendered from the proto at build):

::proto-vocab{axis=quota-metric}

An unregistered bare `Quota.metric` is refused at ingest.

Example: a stock photo with a lifetime cap of 500,000 impressions:
```
quotas: [{ metric: "impressions", limit: 500000, window: TOTAL }]
```

## Obligations

An `Obligation` is a behavioral requirement that applies once the resource is used. Attribution and contribution are obligations, not pricing models.

Each obligation has:
- `kind`: what the agent must do (see the table below).
- `trigger`: when the obligation applies.
- `scope_license`: the license derivatives must be released under, as a `License`. It is required for `SHARE_ALIKE`; set `id` to an SPDX short-id and/or set `uri`. A `uri` requires a `uri_digest`, as on any other `License`.
- `detail`: human-readable text, such as the exact attribution wording or notice text. It is recommended for every kind. The Exchange accepts an `OTHER` obligation without `detail` and reports it in `PushResourcesResponse.warnings`.
- `reporting`: for a `REPORTING` obligation only (rule `obligation.reporting.requires_reporting_kind`), a `ReportingObligation` that states the `window` within which the agent files `ReportUsage` and the `required_fields` the report carries. The term does not set `endpoint` (rule `obligation.reporting.no_endpoint`); the Exchange sets it on the offer. `required` is not read on a term: a `REPORTING` obligation always requires a report. Without `reporting`, the obligation still requires a report, under the Exchange's own window and fields.

::proto-enum{name=ObligationKind label=Kind}

::proto-enum{name=ObligationTrigger label=Trigger}

Because obligations are separate from pricing, any combination is possible: a resource can require attribution **and** charge per access.

**Reporting.** When the term an offer sells carries a `REPORTING` obligation, the Exchange sets `Offer.reporting` from it: `required` is true (rule `offer.reporting.required_by_term`), `window` and `required_fields` are the obligation's where it states them and the Exchange's own where it does not, and `endpoint` is the Exchange's. A term without a `REPORTING` obligation gets the Exchange's own reporting policy. A term whose `Pricing.metering` is `NONE` needs no report, so it cannot carry a `REPORTING` obligation (rule `license_term.reporting.metering_none`).

## Revenue-share arrangements

FORA has no revenue-share pricing model. `PricingModel` has three values, `FREE`, `PER_UNIT` and `FLAT`, and each states a concrete `rate` that the Exchange signs on the offer. A revenue share has no price at transaction time: its rate and its settlement are an agreement between the agent and the publisher outside this protocol.

A revenue-share arrangement is expressed with primitives already in the core:

- A term with `Pricing{model: FREE}`, because there is no per-access charge.
- Gated by an agreement scope, for example `scopes: ["revshare:publisher-x"]`. An agent party to the deal states the matching scope in its signed request (`Requester.scopes`); an agent that does not state it never sees the term.
- A `REPORTING` obligation, so usage is reported. It can state the window and the fields the deal needs, for example a monthly report of `consumed_quantity`, and the Exchange carries it onto the offer's `Offer.reporting`.

A scope gate decides which terms are presented; it is not access control. The Exchange does not verify `Requester.scopes`, so an agent that states the scope without being party to the deal also sees the term. The Exchange records that purchase under the agent's account. To withhold the term from such agents, the publisher needs an Exchange that presents the term only to a requester with a verified delegation granting the scope (see [Scope-Based Access](/protocol/authentication/#scope-based-access)).

So "pay per access **or** enter the revenue-share deal" is two `LicenseTerm`s on the same resource: a public `PER_UNIT` term and a scope-gated `FREE` term. They reach the agent as two offers, and the agent buys one of them. The [academic dataset example](#academic-dataset--seat-quota--share-alike) below has the same shape.

For CoMP interop there is nothing to carry: canonical CoMP V1 has no `revshare` field, and the `fora-comp-v1` profile's keys are flat dotted keys in `ext` (`comp.package_id`, `comp.countries`, …), like every other profile's. See [CoMP extension](/protocol/ext-comp/#ext-fields).

## Metering modes

`Pricing.metering` (field 9, `PricingMetering`) controls how usage is reported after a transaction. It does not change the charge: the charge is fixed at `ExecuteTransaction` in every mode, and a usage report never re-prices a purchase.

::proto-enum{name=PricingMetering label=Mode}

`NONE` suits stock images with perpetual licenses, font licenses, one-time CAD file purchases, and any resource whose whole commercial relationship is settled at the transaction.

`OFFLINE_SELF_REPORTED` suits licenses whose consumption happens outside the digital domain: a design file licensed for manufacturing, a recipe licensed for commercial production, a musical composition licensed for physical pressing.

## Metered pricing: rate, unit and estimate

A `PER_UNIT` price is **metered**: the agent pays per unit. The publisher states the rate and the unit (rule `pricing.per_unit.requires_unit`), and may also state an estimate. A `FLAT` rate is the total fee, with no unit. A `FREE` price has a zero or empty `rate` (rule `pricing.free.zero_rate`).

- `Pricing.estimated_quantity` is the estimate, in the price's `unit`. It is **optional**, on the term the publisher pushes and on the offer. A `PER_UNIT` price may state a rate with or without an estimate; whether to state one is the publisher's decision. An estimate that an offer states is positive (rule `offer.metered.estimate_positive`), so a publisher states a positive estimate. The estimate counts the price's own unit: a price per access has an estimate in accesses, a price per token an estimate in tokens.
- The estimate describes the resource rather than the arrangement, so it can be stated once on `ResourceEntry.estimated_quantity`, and the Exchange puts it on each offer's pricing. An estimate on the term's own pricing takes precedence. When the publisher states none, the offer carries none.

A metered purchase charges estimate × rate, or one unit's rate when the offer states no estimate, and the charge is final. [Charging a metered purchase](/protocol/transaction-flow/#charging-a-metered-purchase) has the rule and an example.

```
pricing: { model: PER_UNIT, unit: "tokens", rate: "0.00002", currency: "USD",
           estimated_quantity: 2500 }   # the purchase charges 2500 x 0.00002
```

## Other term fields

- `LicenseTerm.part_label`: an informational, human-readable name for a sub-part of the resource.
- `License.id`: a stable short identifier, such as an SPDX short-id (`GPL-3.0-only`) or a catalog document id.
- `License.name`: a human-readable name for the license.
- `License.immutable`: the document at `uri` is versioned and will not change.
- `Pricing.license_duration_months`: how long the granted access remains valid, in months.
- `Pricing.unit_cost`: optional. The Exchange's normalized cost per unit, in its base currency, for comparison across Exchanges.

## Cross-domain examples

The examples use short enum names. On the wire they are the full names, for example `TERM_SEMANTICS_ENUMERATED`, `PRICING_MODEL_FREE` and `QUOTA_WINDOW_TOTAL`. Digests are shortened for display; on the wire a digest has its method's full hex length.

### News article — two terms

A tech news publisher offers the same article under two arrangements.

**Term 1**: free for academic RAG:
```
semantics: ENUMERATED
restrictions:
  - kind: FUNCTION, permitted: ["ai-input"], prohibited: ["ai-train"]
  - kind: USER_TYPE, permitted: ["academic"]
obligations:
  - kind: ATTRIBUTION, trigger: ON_USE,
    detail: "Cite Example Tech News at the original URL"
pricing: { model: FREE }
```

**Term 2**: paid, for commercial entities:
```
semantics: ENUMERATED
restrictions:
  - kind: FUNCTION, permitted: ["ai-input", "ai-index"]
  - kind: USER_TYPE, permitted: ["commercial_entity"]
pricing: { model: PER_UNIT, unit: "accesses", rate: "0.05", currency: "USD" }
obligations:
  - kind: ATTRIBUTION, trigger: ON_USE,
    detail: "Cite Example Tech News at the original URL"
```

Neither term has scopes, so every agent receives both offers. An academic RAG agent can honor Term 1's restrictions and chooses it; a commercial legal research platform chooses Term 2. Term 2's offer carries `pricing: { model: PER_UNIT, unit: "accesses", rate: "0.05", currency: "USD" }` with no estimate, so a purchase charges one access, $0.05. The term on that offer carries no pricing. The agent picks the applicable **offer** and executes it.

### Stock photo — impressions quota + perpetual license

A stock photo agency licenses a photo for commercial display:

```
semantics: ENUMERATED
restrictions:
  - kind: FUNCTION, permitted: ["display"]
  - kind: GEOGRAPHY, permitted: ["*"]
pricing: { model: FLAT, rate: "1.20", currency: "USD", metering: NONE }
quotas:
  - { metric: "impressions", limit: 500000, window: TOTAL }
obligations:
  - kind: NOTICE, trigger: ON_USE,
    detail: "© Example Stock Photos. All rights reserved."
```

`metering: NONE` means the agent pays once and the ledger entry closes at `ExecuteTransaction`; no `ReportUsage` is required. The impressions quota is a declared cap that the Exchange does not enforce.

### Patent design file — manufacture function + offline metering

A design licensing firm licenses a 3D-printable part:

```
semantics: ENUMERATED
restrictions:
  - kind: FUNCTION, permitted: ["manufacture"]
  - kind: GEOGRAPHY, permitted: ["*"]
pricing: { model: FLAT, rate: "200.00", currency: "USD",
           metering: OFFLINE_SELF_REPORTED }
quotas:
  - { metric: "units-manufactured", limit: 1000, window: TOTAL }
obligations:
  - kind: NOTICE, trigger: ON_DISTRIBUTION,
    detail: "Manufactured under license from Example Designs"
```

The $200 fee licenses manufacture of up to 1,000 units. The agent reports the units it manufactures with `ReportUsage`; a report never changes the charge. To manufacture more than 1,000 units, the agent needs a new term.

### Licensed music track — complex rights

A music rights holder offers a sync license.

**Term 1**: machine-enumerable sync rights:
```
semantics: ENUMERATED
restrictions:
  - kind: FUNCTION, permitted: ["sync"]
  - kind: GEOGRAPHY, permitted: ["*"]
pricing: { model: FLAT, rate: "500.00", currency: "USD", metering: NONE }
obligations:
  - kind: NOTICE, trigger: ON_DISTRIBUTION,
    detail: "℗ 2024 Example Records. Licensed under Example Collecting Society license 0001."
```

**Term 2**: full collecting-society terms by reference:
```
semantics: REFERENCE_ONLY
license:
  uri: "https://rights.example/licenses/sync-0001"
  name: "Example Collecting Society sync license 0001"
  uri_digest: "sha256:3b1f8e0c…"
pricing: { model: FLAT, rate: "500.00", currency: "USD", metering: NONE }
```

`Pricing` is required even on a `REFERENCE_ONLY` catalog term: the human-readable terms live in the referenced document, but the machine-readable price is always stated. On the offer, it is `Offer.pricing`.

The Exchange presents one offer for each term. Term 1 is complete as enumerated. An agent that buys Term 2 fetches the document at its `License.uri`, checks it against `uri_digest`, and reads it before it uses the track.

### Academic dataset — seat quota + share-alike

A data provider licenses a research dataset:

```
semantics: ENUMERATED
scopes: ["subscription:institution"]
restrictions:
  - kind: FUNCTION, permitted: ["ai-train", "ai-input"]
  - kind: USER_TYPE, permitted: ["academic", "non_profit"]
pricing: { model: FREE }
quotas:
  - { metric: "seats", limit: 10, window: MONTHLY }
obligations:
  - kind: SHARE_ALIKE, trigger: ON_DISTRIBUTION,
    scope_license: { id: "CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0" },
    detail: "Trained models using this dataset must be released under CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0"
```

The institutional subscription is settled off-protocol. On-protocol, the Exchange presents the term to an agent whose request states the `subscription:institution` scope; the term is `model: FREE` because there is no per-access charge. An Exchange that requires proof presents the term only when a verified delegation chain grants the scope. The chain is issued by the institution and bound to the agent's key, so the Exchange can verify it only on a request the agent signs itself, and a query a Broker originates never yields the term. Up to 10 named users a month can use the dataset. A model trained on it must be released under CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 when it is distributed.

## Vocabulary registry

The open vocabulary axes are **proto-native**: each registered token list is authored once, as buf custom options directly on the field or enum value that uses it, with no side-car JSON registry:

- function, geography and user-type tokens: `(fora.v1.vocab_enum)` on the `RESTRICTION_KIND_FUNCTION`, `RESTRICTION_KIND_GEOGRAPHY` and `RESTRICTION_KIND_USER_TYPE` enum values
- quota metrics: `(fora.v1.vocab)` on `Quota.metric`
- pricing units: `(fora.v1.vocab)` on `Pricing.unit`

The `protoc-gen-foravocab` buf plugin reads these options and generates a typed constant package per axis (`functiontokens`, `geographytokens`, `usertypes`, `quotametrics`, `pricingunits`) with an `IsRegistered` membership check, an `Aliases` map and a `Canonical` lookup. The constants, the membership check, the aliases and the validation all derive from the single authored list. Aliases are `(fora.v1.vocab_enum_alias)` entries beside the tokens (`train-ai=ai-train`, `generative-ai=ai-input`, `scrape=crawl`, `tdm=text-and-data-mining`, `copy=reproduce`, `adapt=modify`, `derivative=modify`; `personal=individual`, `business=commercial_entity`, `enterprise=commercial_entity`). Geography has no aliases and registers only the non-ISO specials (`*`, `EU`, `EEA`); country codes are ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes.

Unregistered bare **restriction** tokens produce `PushResourcesResponse.warnings[]` entries at ingest and do not cause rejection. The metering fields are different: an unregistered bare `Pricing.unit` or `Quota.metric` is refused, because the metering basis is what an agent is charged or capped by, and no transactable offer can be made on an unknown one.
